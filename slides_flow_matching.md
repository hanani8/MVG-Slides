# Neural Codec Language Models

They leverage **discrete audio tokens** generated by **neural codecs** to model and generate high-quality audio by training **LLMs** to predict **audio tokens** conditioned on **text tokens**.

The audio tokens are _decoded to waveform_ using the **decoder** part of the _codec_.

They are highly _natural_, _expressive_, and possess zero-shot capabilities. (Abstract, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.05407)

## Representation Problem

1 token per each segment of audio is not terribly good at representing acoustic details.

## Solution

Generate multiple tokens per segment of audio

## Latency Ballooning Problem

To generate one segment of audio, multiple autoregressive steps need to take place. This causes increase in Latency.

### Moshi-Example

Segments are sampled at 12.5Hz (e.g. per second)
8 tokens per segment.
To keep up with real-time, you need $12.5 \times 8 = 100 \text{ tokens per sec}$
Therefore, we are forced to use small transformer models, if we want real-time audio.

# Joining Autoregressive Decoders & DDPMs

## (Section 2.1, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.07243)

* Autoregressive models are strong at converting between unaligned domains like vision, text
and speech.
*  DDPMs operate in the continuous domain which allows them to model expressive modalities
* These models (Diffusion Models) have been shown to be quite effective at using low-quality guidance signals to reconstruct the high-dimensional space that those guidance signals were derived from. 
* Put another way, they (Diffusion Models) are great at super-resolution.

# Flow-Matching: An efficient Diffusion

Flow-Matching models are easier to train and faster at inference time compared to Diffusion Models. (Introduction, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.05407)

layout: default

# Flow-Matching

A generative framework that learns to transport a simple distribution (Gaussian noise) to a complex one (mel-spectrograms) by regressing a velocity vector field

## Training Objective & Loss

The goal is to learn a vector field $v_\theta$ that matches a predefined probability path between noise $x_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ and data $x_1 \sim \mathcal{P}_{data}$

### The Flow-Matching Loss ($L_{FM}$):
The loss is a simple Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted vector field and the ground-truth velocity

$$L_{FM} = \mathbb{E}_{t, x_0, x_1} [\| v_\theta(x_t, t \mid c) - (x_1 - x_0) \|^2]$$
